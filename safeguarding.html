The Foundation

Safeguarding Policy

Last reviewed: October 2026 · Reviewed before each intake

Our commitment

Every child who joins The Foundation will be safe, respected and supported to grow, on the pitch and in the classroom. Parents, guardians, coaches and teachers trust us with young people, and this policy sets out exactly what that trust means in practice.

The Foundation exists to help young rugby players chase the game without sacrificing their education. A child cannot grow where they do not feel safe, so safeguarding comes before every drill, session and target.

Who this policy covers

This policy applies to every adult involved in running The Foundation: the founder, any assistant coaches or volunteers, guest speakers, and partner coaches and teachers while they work with the program.

It covers every Foundation activity: training sessions, talk sessions, tutoring and study groups, travel to and from sessions, and all communication with players, whether in person, by phone or online.

A child is anyone under 18. Where a campus player is 18 or older, the same standards of respect and conduct still apply.

Our principles

  • The child comes first. When a decision affects a child's safety or wellbeing, that decision wins over everything else, including results and schedules.
  • Every child is protected equally. Age, gender, background, ability, religion and playing level make no difference to the care a child receives.
  • Education is protected. No child is asked to miss school, exams or study time to take part.
  • Nothing is hidden. Parents, guardians, coaches and teachers can always ask what happens in sessions, and they will get a straight answer.
  • Everyone shares the duty. Safeguarding is the responsibility of every adult in the program, not one person.

Code of conduct for adults

Every adult working with The Foundation agrees to the following.

We will:

  • Treat every child with respect, patience and dignity.
  • Keep sessions open and visible. Adults are never alone with a single child out of sight of others.
  • Give praise and correction in a way that builds a player up, never humiliates them.
  • Set a good example in language, behaviour and sportsmanship.
  • Report any concern straight away, however small it seems.

We will never:

  • Hit, shove, threaten or shout abusively at a child, or use punishment that is physical or degrading.
  • Touch a child in a way that is not needed for coaching or first aid, and we will explain a technique before demonstrating it on a child.
  • Make comments of a sexual nature, or take part in any relationship with a child beyond that of mentor and player.
  • Give a child alcohol, tobacco, drugs or supplements, or be under the influence of any of them ourselves in a session.
  • Offer gifts, favours or special treatment to one child in return for secrecy, or ask a child to keep a secret from their parents or guardians.
  • Transport a child alone in a private vehicle.

Any adult who breaks this code will be removed from the program while the matter is looked into.

Keeping sessions physically safe

Rugby is a contact sport, and The Foundation treats that seriously.

  • Venues: sessions are held at schools, clubs or grounds that the host has agreed to, and the pitch and equipment are checked before each session.
  • Supervision: at least two adults are present at every session, and the group is always supervised by someone who knows where every child is.
  • Age-appropriate training: the program is built around conditioning, core work, handling and decision-making. Contact work is limited, introduced gradually and taught properly, in line with the safety rules for the age group.
  • First aid: a first aid kit is at every session, and at least one adult present knows basic first aid. Parents are told about any injury the same day.
  • Concussion: any player with a suspected head injury stops immediately, is not allowed to continue that day, and is only cleared to return after medical advice. We follow the "if in doubt, sit them out" rule without exception.
  • Health and readiness: guardians tell us about any condition we should know of before the first session. Players are never pushed to train through pain, illness or exhaustion.
  • Emergencies: every session has a named adult responsible for emergencies and a way to reach guardians and emergency services immediately.

Protecting education and wellbeing

Safe growth means looking after the whole child, not only the athlete.

  • School comes first. Sessions are scheduled around lessons, exams and homework. A player who needs time for study or exams is encouraged to take it.
  • Academics are part of the program. Players check in on attendance and progress, hold each other accountable, and support one another through peer-led tutoring, where students strong in a subject help those who are not.
  • Character is coached. Talk sessions cover resilience, teamwork and heart, and every player is asked how they are doing off the pitch as well as on it.
  • Pressure is managed. No child is shamed for a loss, an injury, a poor result or a decision to step back. Playing time and selection are explained fairly.
  • Everyone belongs. Bullying, teasing, hazing and exclusion are not tolerated, among players or adults.
  • Concerns are shared, with care. If a player seems unwell, stressed or struggling, we speak to the guardian and, where it helps, the school, so the child gets the right support.

Communication, photos and social media

  • Messages go through guardians or groups. For players under 18, adults do not message a child privately. Contact goes through a guardian or through a group that includes a guardian or a second adult.
  • Keep it about the program. Messages cover sessions, schedules and schoolwork. Adults do not connect with players under 18 on personal social media accounts.
  • Photos and video need permission. We only photograph or film players with a guardian's written consent. Consent can be withdrawn at any time, and we will remove the content.
  • Protecting identity. We do not post a child's home address, school timetable or phone number, and we do not tag a child's location in real time.
  • Respectful content only. Anything we share shows players in appropriate dress and in a positive light.

Consent and what guardians can expect

No child under 18 joins without a guardian's written consent. Before the first session, every family receives:

  • A clear description of what the program involves, where and when sessions take place, and who will be present.
  • A consent form, including emergency contacts and any health information we should know about.
  • A copy of this policy and the name of the person to contact with questions or concerns.

During the program, guardians can expect to:

  • Be told promptly about any injury, incident or concern involving their child.
  • Hear how their child is progressing, on the pitch and in school.
  • Visit or observe a session, with notice.
  • Withdraw their child at any time, for any reason, without being questioned or penalised.

Raising a concern and how we respond

Anyone can raise a concern: a child, a parent or guardian, a coach, a teacher, a volunteer or a member of the public. You do not need proof. If something feels wrong, tell us.

How to raise it: contact the Safeguarding Lead, Cantona Eric Ouko, by WhatsApp on +254 797 243 706 or by email at cantonaericouko@gmail.com. If the concern is about the Safeguarding Lead, or you would rather not go through us, contact Childline Kenya on 116 or the police directly.

What happens next:

  1. We listen, write down what was said in the person's own words, and keep it confidential.
  2. If a child is in immediate danger, we act at once: the child is kept safe, and we contact the police or emergency services.
  3. Any adult named in a concern is kept away from children in the program while it is looked into.
  4. Serious concerns are reported to the right authorities, such as the police or the Children's Office, and to the child's school or club where appropriate.
  5. The family is kept informed of what we are doing, unless telling them would put the child at risk.
  6. We record what happened and what we decided, and we learn from it.

No one who raises a concern in good faith will be blamed or penalised for doing so.

If a child tells an adult about harm, that adult listens calmly, does not promise to keep it secret, does not question the child for details, and passes it on to the Safeguarding Lead the same day.

In immediate danger? Call 999 or 112. Childline Kenya, 116, is a free helpline for children and anyone worried about a child.

The role of partner coaches and teachers

Coaches and teachers are partners in The Foundation, not bystanders. They know their players best, and the program works only when everyone watches out for the same children.

What partners can expect from us:

  • Advance notice of session plans, dates and venues.
  • A named contact for any question or concern about a student.
  • Feedback on how their players are doing in the program.
  • Respect for school and club rules, discipline and timetables.

What we ask of partners:

  • Share anything that helps us look after a child, such as a health need or a difficult week, with the guardian's knowledge.
  • Tell us straight away if a student's attendance, grades or wellbeing starts to slip.
  • Follow the same code of conduct when working with Foundation players during program activities.
  • Raise any concern through the process above, and your own school or club procedures where these apply.

Review and contacts

This policy is reviewed before each intake and after any serious incident. Parents, guardians, coaches, teachers and players are welcome to suggest improvements at any time.
